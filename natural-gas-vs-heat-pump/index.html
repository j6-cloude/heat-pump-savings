Natural gas vs heat pump

By j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

Natural gas is the cheapest heat in most states this winter. EIA expects gas-heated homes to spend about $640 this winter, 9% less than last winter. A heat pump beats gas mainly where electricity is cheap or gas is costly.

A therm of gas is 100,000 Btu. A 90% furnace delivers 90,000 Btu. A heat pump with COP 2.5 uses about 10.6 kWh to make that heat. So at $1.50 a therm, the heat pump wins only if power costs under about $0.14 per kWh. Many people still choose a heat pump to replace an old air conditioner, since it cools too.

Words on this page

  • Heat pump: an electric system that moves heat from outside air into your home. It also cools in summer.
  • COP: how many units of heat you get for each unit of electricity. COP 2.5 means 2.5 times more heat than a space heater on the same power.
  • HSPF2: a heat pump's heating rating over a season. Higher is better. HSPF2 ÷ 3.412 ≈ average COP in a mild climate.
  • Btu: a unit of heat. One gallon of heating oil holds about 138,500 Btu (EIA).

Questions people ask

Will a heat pump lower my gas bill?

It removes the gas heating bill but adds electricity. In many states the total is about the same or higher at today's prices. Check yours.

What is a dual-fuel system?

A heat pump plus a gas furnace. The heat pump heats on mild days, and the furnace takes over when it's very cold or when gas is cheaper.
