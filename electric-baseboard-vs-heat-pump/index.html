Electric baseboard vs heat pump

By j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

A heat pump uses about 50–70% less electricity than baseboard heat for the same warmth, because it moves heat instead of making it. Baseboards have a COP of 1. A heat pump with COP 2.5 uses 60% less.

This is the switch that still qualifies for federal HEAR rebates after Sept 1, 2026: replacing electric equipment with more efficient electric equipment (DOE Program Notice 26-2), where your state has opened its program.

Words on this page

  • Heat pump: an electric system that moves heat from outside air into your home. It also cools in summer.
  • COP: how many units of heat you get for each unit of electricity. COP 2.5 means 2.5 times more heat than a space heater on the same power.
  • HSPF2: a heat pump's heating rating over a season. Higher is better. HSPF2 ÷ 3.412 ≈ average COP in a mild climate.
  • Btu: a unit of heat. One gallon of heating oil holds about 138,500 Btu (EIA).

Questions people ask

How much does a heat pump save over baseboard heat?

With COP 2.5, about 60% of the heating electricity. With COP 2, about 50%. The calculator shows dollars for your state.

Are ductless mini-splits good for baseboard homes?

Yes. Many baseboard homes have no ducts, so mini-splits are a common fit.
