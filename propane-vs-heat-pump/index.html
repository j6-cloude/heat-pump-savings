Propane vs heat pump

By j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

Propane averages $2.69 a gallon in the U.S. (EIA, week of 2026-10-19), but prices vary a lot: from about $1.65 in North Dakota to about $4.93 in Florida (state prices EIA surveys). Where propane is costly, a heat pump usually wins. In the Midwest, where propane is cheap, it can be close.

One gallon of propane holds about 91,452 Btu (EIA). A 90% furnace delivers about 82,000 Btu. A heat pump with COP 2.5 makes that with about 9.6 kWh. So the heat pump wins if your power costs less than about $0.28 per kWh at the U.S. average propane price.

Words on this page

  • Heat pump: an electric system that moves heat from outside air into your home. It also cools in summer.
  • COP: how many units of heat you get for each unit of electricity. COP 2.5 means 2.5 times more heat than a space heater on the same power.
  • HSPF2: a heat pump's heating rating over a season. Higher is better. HSPF2 ÷ 3.412 ≈ average COP in a mild climate.
  • Btu: a unit of heat. One gallon of heating oil holds about 138,500 Btu (EIA).

Questions people ask

Is propane or a heat pump cheaper?

It depends on your state. With propane above about $3 a gallon, a heat pump usually costs less to run. With propane under $2, it is often close.

How many kWh equals a gallon of propane?

About 26.8 kWh of energy (91,452 Btu ÷ 3,412). With a COP 2.5 heat pump, about 9.6 kWh makes the same useful heat as a gallon burned at 90%.
