Heat pump rebates and credits in 2026

By j6 · Updated October 28, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

Short answer: the federal 30% heat pump tax credit (25C, up to $2,000 a year) is gone for equipment placed in service after Dec 31, 2025. Federal Home Energy Rebates still exist in some states, but since Sept 1, 2026 they can't pay to replace oil, propane or gas heat with a heat pump unless you keep the old system. State and utility rebates are separate and may still help.

If you heat with oil, propane or gas

Under DOE's new guidance, a HEAR rebate can still help with a heat pump if the existing fossil-fuel system stays in place and is not taken out. The heat pump no longer has to be your main heat (NASEO FAQ, Sept 2026). Rebates reserved on or before Aug 31, 2026 follow the old rules.

If you heat with electric baseboards or an old heat pump

This is the main case HEAR covers now: replacing electric equipment with more efficient electric equipment.

How to check what you can get

  1. Check your state energy office page for HEAR/HOMES status (the ENERGY STAR HEAR page lists state programs).
  2. Check your electric utility's rebate page. These are often the biggest rebates left.
  3. Ask installers which rebates they process. Get the rebate amount in writing before you sign.
  4. Use our payback calculator with the quote and rebate.

Watch out: some ads still promise a "30% federal tax credit" on heat pumps. That credit doesn't apply to systems installed in 2026.

Questions people ask

Is there a federal tax credit for heat pumps in 2026?

No. The IRS says the 25C credit does not apply to property placed in service after December 31, 2025.

Can I get a HEAR rebate to replace my oil furnace with a heat pump?

Not for new reservations since Sept 1, 2026, unless the oil system stays in place. Rules come from DOE Program Notice 26-2; your state runs the program.

What is the biggest heat pump rebate left?

It depends on where you live. Check your electric utility and your state energy office first.
