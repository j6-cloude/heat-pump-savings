How it works

By j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

The steps

  1. Heat your home needs per year. Home-size mode: square feet × your state's heating degree days × 3, 4 or 5.5 Btu (good, average, drafty insulation). Usage mode: last winter's fuel × its heat content × your system's efficiency.
  2. Cost now: heat needed ÷ (fuel heat content × efficiency) × price.
  3. Heat pump cost: heat needed ÷ 3,412 Btu/kWh ÷ COP × electricity price.
  4. COP: the COP you enter, or HSPF2 ÷ 3.412, adjusted for climate: ×1.05 below 3,000 heating degree days, ×1.0 for 3,000–5,000, ×0.9 for 5,000–7,000, ×0.8 above 7,000.

Prices

Prices: electricity = Residential average, January–August 2026 (August 2026 YTD) (EIA Table 5.6.B). Natural gas = Residential average of monthly prices, November 2025–March 2026 (last heating season) (EIA). Heating oil and propane = EIA weekly residential price, week of 2026-10-19; states EIA doesn't survey use the regional or U.S. price. Climate = NOAA heating degree days. You can type your own prices.

Why these periods? Electricity uses the newest year-to-date average so it includes winter months. Gas uses last winter's months because summer gas prices per unit are much higher and don't reflect heating bills. Oil and propane use EIA's newest weekly survey, which runs October through March.

Our assumptions (not official numbers)

The heat-loss factors and the climate adjustment for COP are our own planning assumptions, chosen to give typical results. They are not from a government source. Usage mode, with your real fuel use, is more accurate.

Limits

  • Running costs only. No purchase, install, maintenance or demand charges.
  • Doesn't model backup heat strips, dual-fuel or time-of-use rates.
  • State averages hide big local differences. Use your own bill prices when you can.
