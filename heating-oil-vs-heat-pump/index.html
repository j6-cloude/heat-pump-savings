Heating oil vs heat pump

By j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

Heating oil costs about $6.19 a gallon in the U.S. (EIA, week of 2026-10-19). EIA expects oil-heated homes to spend about $2,115 this winter, 21% more than last winter. In most states a heat pump costs less to run than an oil furnace or boiler.

One gallon of oil gives about 138,500 Btu of heat, and an 83% efficient boiler delivers about 115,000 Btu. To make the same heat, a heat pump with a COP of 2.5 uses about 13.5 kWh. So if your electricity costs less than $0.46 per kWh, the heat pump wins. In Massachusetts, our example home would spend $2,610 on oil and $1,936 with a heat pump.

Many oil homes keep the boiler as a backup for the coldest days. That is allowed under the new federal HEAR rules (see rebates).

Words on this page

  • Heat pump: an electric system that moves heat from outside air into your home. It also cools in summer.
  • COP: how many units of heat you get for each unit of electricity. COP 2.5 means 2.5 times more heat than a space heater on the same power.
  • HSPF2: a heat pump's heating rating over a season. Higher is better. HSPF2 ÷ 3.412 ≈ average COP in a mild climate.
  • Btu: a unit of heat. One gallon of heating oil holds about 138,500 Btu (EIA).

Questions people ask

How many kWh equals a gallon of heating oil?

A gallon of heating oil holds about 138,500 Btu (EIA), which equals about 40.6 kWh of electricity. A heat pump with COP 2.5 makes the useful heat of a gallon burned at 83% efficiency with about 13.5 kWh.

Should I keep my oil boiler?

Many people keep it as backup for very cold days. You can choose that in your plan. Since Sept 1, 2026, keeping the old system is required if you want a federal HEAR rebate for the heat pump.

Why did oil heat get more expensive this winter?

EIA's Winter Fuels Outlook expects higher heating oil prices this winter. Weekly residential oil was $6.19 a gallon on 2026-10-19.
