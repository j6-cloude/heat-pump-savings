Heat pump savings in New York

By j6 · Updated October 27, 2026 · Prices from EIA (dates on each page) · Estimates, not quotes

Short answer for New York: at this winter's prices, a heat pump costs less to run than heating oil, propane and electric baseboard in a typical 2,000 sq ft home. Electricity here averages 29.26¢ per kWh, 61% above the U.S. average of 18.19¢ (#47 cheapest of 51).

Rebates in New York

The federal 25C tax credit for heat pumps ended for equipment placed in service after Dec 31, 2025 (IRS). Federal Home Energy Rebates (HEAR/HOMES) are run by each state, and many states have not opened. Since Sept 1, 2026, new HEAR rebates can't pay to replace oil, propane or gas heat unless the old system stays in place (DOE Program Notice 26-2). Check your state energy office and your electric utility. Full 2026 rebate guide.

Questions people ask

Is a heat pump cheaper than oil in New York?

In our example home, oil costs about $2,680 a year and a heat pump about $1,836. The heat pump wins if your electricity costs less than $0.427 per kWh.

Is a heat pump cheaper than natural gas in New York?

In our example, gas costs about $879 a year and a heat pump about $1,836. The break-even electricity price is $0.140 per kWh.

What does electricity cost in New York?

29.26 cents per kWh on average for homes, January–August 2026 (EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.B). Your utility's rate may differ.
