The short answer

If you heat with oil or propane, a heat pump usually costs less to run. If you heat with natural gas, it often costs about the same or more, unless your electricity is cheap. If you heat with electric baseboards, a heat pump almost always costs less.

Why? Heating oil costs about $6.19 a gallon in the U.S. this month (EIA, week of 2026-10-19). EIA expects homes that heat with oil to spend about $2,115 this winter (Nov–Mar), up 21%. Homes on natural gas: about $640, down 9% (EIA Winter Fuels Outlook).

Words on this page

  • Heat pump: an electric system that moves heat from outside air into your home. It also cools in summer.
  • COP: how many units of heat you get for each unit of electricity. COP 2.5 means 2.5 times more heat than a space heater on the same power.
  • HSPF2: a heat pump's heating rating over a season. Higher is better. HSPF2 ÷ 3.412 ≈ average COP in a mild climate.
  • Btu: a unit of heat. One gallon of heating oil holds about 138,500 Btu (EIA).

Questions people ask

Does a heat pump save money over oil?

Usually yes. At $6.19 a gallon (EIA U.S. average, week of 2026-10-19), oil heat costs much more per unit of heat than a heat pump in most states. Check your state's electricity price in the calculator.

Does a heat pump save money over natural gas?

Often not by much. Gas is cheap in many states this winter. A heat pump can still win where electricity is cheap or where you'd also replace an air conditioner.

Do heat pumps work in cold climates?

Yes, but they make less heat and use more power when it is very cold. Cold-climate models keep working below 0°F. Our calculator lowers the efficiency for colder states.

Is there still a federal tax credit for heat pumps?

No. The 25C credit (30%, up to $2,000) does not apply to equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025, per the IRS. Some state and utility rebates still exist.
